## Do I need a commercial license?

| What you are doing | License you need |
|---|---|
| Learning, evaluating, experimenting | GPL-3.0 |
| Internal tool used only inside your company, never distributed | GPL-3.0 |
| Open-source project that is itself GPL-3.0 compatible | GPL-3.0 |
| Academic research and publications | GPL-3.0 |
| Selling or distributing a product that includes [PROJECT NAME] and whose source you do **not** publish | **Commercial** |
| Shipping firmware or a device containing [PROJECT NAME] with closed source | **Commercial** |
| SaaS where you distribute a client binary containing [PROJECT NAME] | **Commercial** |
| Your company policy or a customer contract forbids GPL code | **Commercial** |
| You need a warranty, indemnification, or a signed contract | **Commercial** |

**Rule of thumb:** if you ship it and you keep your source closed, you need the commercial license.

## FAQ

**Is the GPL version a limited or crippled build?**
No. Both licenses cover exactly the same code. The only difference is the legal terms.

**We use it internally and never distribute it. Do we owe anything?**
No. The GPL's obligations are triggered by distribution, not by use. Internal use is free.

**We link to it dynamically. Does that avoid the GPL?**
No. GPL-3.0 is a strong copyleft license; dynamic linking does not exempt you. (That is a deliberate choice — the LGPL, which does allow this, is not used here.)

**We already shipped under the GPL. Can we buy our way out retroactively?**
A commercial license applies from its effective date forward. Rights already granted to your users under the GPL cannot be withdrawn. Contact us to discuss the transition.

**Can we get the source code?**
Yes, with either license. The commercial license simply removes the obligation to publish yours.

**Can we redistribute [PROJECT NAME] on its own?**
Under the GPL, yes, on GPL terms. Under the commercial license, no — it may only ship embedded in your product.
